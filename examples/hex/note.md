# Hex

- **Source:** https://hex.tech (found via navbar.gallery), captured 2026-11-05 at 1440×900 and iPhone 13 (390×664)
- **Type:** landing page (SaaS, data analytics)
- **Take from it:** a split nav with the logo in the middle, framed by crop marks; the main button drawn as a selected object with corner handles; inline product "chips" inside the subheadline; a sans + italic serif headline pair; live product mockups with prompt chips that type
- **Related guideline:** `guidelines/navigation-and-footers.md`, `guidelines/typography.md`, `guidelines/landing-pages.md`
- **Don't take:** the floating "Ask natural language questions / Learn about Threads" toast that sits over the content on every screen

Fonts: PP Formula SemiExtended (700) for headings, PP Editorial New italic (200) for the expressive words, Cinetype and IBM Plex for text and mono labels. Warm grey paper (`#f5f2f0`-ish) with a dot and line grid, near-black aubergine text, a soft pink accent.

## Navbar

- **Split around a centered logo:** Platform ▾, Solutions ▾, Enterprise on the left of the HEX wordmark; Resources ▾, Pricing, Log In and "Get started" on the right. 14px, regular weight.
- **Crop marks:** the nav area is boxed by thin lines with small corner ticks, like print registration marks. Sections below use the same ticks, so the page feels like a technical drawing.
- **"Get started" has selection handles:** small circles on its four corners, like a selected frame in a design tool. On hover the handles go and the button settles to a plain outlined box. The hero's "Get started for free" uses the same handles.
- **Hover:** each nav item gets a light grey box behind it and its chevron flips up.
- **Mega menu:** a wide white panel, two columns (Products, Capabilities), each item an icon, a name and one plain-language line ("Ask and explore data in plain language"), plus a featured tile with a line-art background at the bottom.
- **Phone:** wordmark left, a round button with two lines on the right.

## Hero

- Headline in two voices: "The Analytics platform" in a semi-extended bold sans, "*for humans and agents*" in a thin italic serif. The serif is the human part.
- Small pink sparkle lines burst from the end of the headline.
- **Chips in the sentence:** "Rethinking [📊 BI] [💬 Conversational analytics] and [⟳ Context management] for the agentic era." Each product name is a pill with its icon inside the line of text. The subheadline doubles as a product menu.
- Two outlined buttons, then "TRUSTED BY LEADING DATA COMPANIES" between bracket marks, and two rows of logos. Some logos carry a tiny dotted ornament.

## Scroll and sections

- **Orbits:** "A different approach…" sits among dotted curved lines with AI-tool icons (Claude, OpenAI, Slack…) and question marks travelling along them.
- **Real product, alive:** every feature has a working-looking UI: a chat with the agent that "thinks for 11 seconds", a chart building, Slack threads, a CLI. Dashboard cards have prompt chips under them ("Show demand as a heatmap ↵") with a typing cursor.
- **Quote beside each feature:** short customer quote, logo, name and role in mono. Proof attached to the claim it supports.
- **Isometric line art** for "Flexible by design" and "Control without compromise": thin black drawings, no color.
- **Italic serif as an accent** in section heads: "*Loved* by the best data teams", "*Instant integration* with the whole stack", "Use cases / *Get the most out of Hex*".
- G2 badges shown as a cluster, next to a big Notion quote.

## Footer

- Plain on the same paper: wordmark with "Made with 🍵 on 🌍", four columns (Platform, Resources, Company, Connect), legal row.
- **A mechanical counter** ("2004553") sits in the top-right corner of the footer in a segmented display font. A small, living detail.

## Phone

- Headline stacks with the italic serif lines; the chips wrap one per line, which makes them look like a menu.
- Logos go two per row. Product mockups are cropped at the right edge instead of shrunk, so they stay readable.

## What to learn

- Mixing a bold sans with a thin italic serif lets one headline carry two tones (machine / human).
- Put product names in chips inside the copy: one sentence explains the offer and links to it.
- Give the main CTA a detail from the product's world (selection handles for a tool people build things in).
- Crop marks and a faint grid make a page feel engineered without adding color.
