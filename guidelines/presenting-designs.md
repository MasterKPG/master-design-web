# Presenting Designs

A design is only half done until it's presented. Use this when making portfolio shots, social posts, client decks or case studies, not when building the product itself.

## Showcase (portfolio and social)

1. **Plain background color.** The most basic and minimal. In light mode, take the accent color, darken it and desaturate it toward grey (bright colors would out-pop the design), then add a faint shadow under the UI.
2. **Dark mode glow.** Large blurred circles of the accent color behind the UI, so the design seems to emit light. Optionally a glassy background with a soft gradient stroke on the edges.
3. **Minimalism works almost always.** Bland backgrounds make the design pop.
4. **Explode the image.** Extend the UI's lines or patterns past the frame, let the dashboard run off the screen, or extend the brand's visual identity with key metrics around it.
5. **Skew.** Just enough (e.g. 2° vertical, -14° horizontal). Pop one element out with an offset and a shadow; a grey placeholder where it was can strengthen the effect.
6. **Collage of screens.** Offset and slide screens off the page; add more screens to fill space; don't line everything up on a grid; add a few degrees of rotation, or skew the whole collage; pop out the most important frame.
   - Large designs (landing pages) suit collages best. For dashboards, zoom into one section and animate it slightly.
7. **Images.** Hardest to balance. Best for physical products (a coffee shop, a car). For software, fluid gradient images sometimes work, but the methods above usually work better.

## Professional (clients)

- The goal isn't to wow the client. It's to **build confidence in the product.** Show it in a realistic context.
- **Mockups:** even a plain computer frame helps. A lifestyle mockup (the UI in use on an iPad or Apple Watch) elevates it further. The latest Apple product is the gold standard.
- **Custom AI mockups:** AI isn't good at thoughtful UI, but it's good at mockups. Prompt with specifics (device, green screen, surface, background, lighting, "lived in" details), punch out the green screen and skew the design in. Small details sell it, just as in UI.
- High-quality product mockups are critical for e-commerce designs.

## Prototype: show, don't describe

- A working prototype lets people *feel* the experience: hidden swipe actions, how a modal animates in, the sequence when a link is deleted.
- These small details communicate polish that static images can't.
