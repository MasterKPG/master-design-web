On Pine Street, a 115-Year-Old Law Firm Learns to Lend Like a Fund

As banks pulled back from leveraged loans, Varick & Thorne rebuilt its finance group around the direct lenders that replaced them.

By Rebecca Lowell

When the large banks stepped away from leveraged lending, the lawyers on the ninth floor of 27 Pine Street did not wait for them to come back. Within two years, the firm was representing four of the ten largest direct lenders in the country on deals the banks once owned.

Partners there still mark up credit agreements by hand before a closing, a habit the firm says catches the clauses that software misses. Clients describe a practice that is small by Wall Street standards and fast by any standard.
